# Story Game Design – The Final Week in Jerusalem

## Game Premise
The player is a teenage apprentice scribe living in Jerusalem during the final week before Jesus is arrested and put on trial.

Rumours are spreading everywhere:
- Some people say Jesus is a prophet.
- Others say He is the Messiah.
- Religious leaders think He is dangerous.
- Witnesses claim they saw miracles.

The player decides to investigate.

Your goal is to:
- Gather witness testimonies
- Discover evidence
- Listen to Jesus teach
- Understand His parables
- Decide for yourself who Jesus really is

The game combines:
- Exploration
- Dialogue choices
- Quest solving
- Evidence collection
- Moral decisions
- Relationship building

The tone should feel adventurous, emotional, thoughtful, and accessible to teenagers.

---

# Core Gameplay Loop

1. Explore Jerusalem
2. Hear rumours and pick up quests
3. Talk to witnesses
4. Complete tasks/actions
5. Collect evidence items
6. Unlock deeper conversations
7. Attend public teachings
8. Learn meanings of parables
9. Build an investigation board
10. Reach your conclusion before the trial

---

# Main Evidence System

Players collect "Evidence Tokens".

Examples:
- Witness statements
- Fragments of teachings
- Roman reports
- Temple rumours
- Personal objects
- Drawings/maps
- Miracle accounts

Each piece updates the player's Investigation Board.

Possible categories:
- Miracles
- Teachings
- Prophecies
- Public Reaction
- Opposition
- Questions

# QUESTS

# QUEST 1 – "The Donkey King"

## Story Setup
The player hears crowds shouting as Jesus enters Jerusalem riding a donkey.

## Objective
Find out why the crowd treats Him like a king.

## Player Actions
- Follow the crowd
- Collect palm branches
- Speak to witnesses
- Observe Jesus entering the city
- Ask why a donkey matters
- Record crowd reactions

## Evidence Collected
- "Prophecy Fulfilled"
- "Crowd Calls Him King"

## NPC Witnesses
- Excited child
- Elderly pilgrim
- Suspicious merchant
- Roman guard

---

## Dummy Inky Conversation – Excited Child

=== start ===

Player: Why is everyone shouting?

Child: Don't you know? It's Jesus!

+ [Who is Jesus?]
    Child: He healed my cousin in Galilee!
    Child: People say He could be the promised King.

+ [Why the palm branches?]
    Child: We wave them for heroes and kings!

+ [Why the donkey?]
    Child: Someone said a prophet promised the king would come riding one.

Player: Do you believe it?

Child: I don't know... but people seem full of hope.

-> END

---

## Teen Explanation – Prophecy of the Donkey

Long ago, prophets said God's chosen king would come humbly, riding on a donkey instead of a war horse.

Jesus entering Jerusalem this way was like saying:

"I am a different kind of king."

Not a conqueror.
Not violent.
A king of peace.

# QUEST 2 – "The Cleansed Temple"

## Story Setup
Chaos erupts at the Temple after Jesus overturns tables.

## Objective
Discover why Jesus became angry.

## Player Actions
- Investigate overturned stalls
- Speak to merchants
- Find scattered coins
- Talk to poor worshippers
- Avoid angry guards
- Decide whether to return stolen money

## Evidence Collected
- "Temple Corruption"
- "Jesus Defends Worship"

---

## Dummy Inky Conversation – Merchant

=== start ===

Merchant: Look at this mess!

Player: What happened here?

Merchant: That teacher from Galilee stormed in shouting.

+ [Why was he angry?]
    Merchant: He said God's house had become a den of thieves.

+ [Were people cheating?]
    Merchant: Everyone takes a little extra coin. That's business.

+ [Do you think he was right?]
    Merchant: ...Some people were desperate. Maybe things went too far.

Player: Did the crowd support him?

Merchant: More than I expected.

-> END

---

## Teen Explanation – Why Jesus Cleansed the Temple

The Temple was supposed to be a place where people connected with God.

But some leaders and merchants were using religion to make money from poor people.

Jesus became angry because:
- worship had become unfair
- greed was taking over
- people were being exploited

This showed Jesus cared about justice, not just rules.

# QUEST 3 – "The Man Born Blind"

## Story Setup
A beggar claims Jesus healed a man born blind.

## Objective
Find the healed man and verify the story.

## Player Actions
- Search the lower market district
- Follow clues from neighbours
- Compare conflicting stories
- Decide whether witnesses are trustworthy

## Evidence Collected
- "Healing Testimony"
- "Religious Leaders Question Miracle"

---

## Dummy Inky Conversation – Healed Man

=== start ===

Player: People say you were blind.

Man: I was.

+ [What happened?]
    Man: Jesus touched my eyes.
    Man: The next thing I saw was sunlight.

+ [Did everyone believe you?]
    Man: No. Some leaders accused me of lying.

+ [What do you think about Jesus?]
    Man: I don't know everything.
    Man: But nobody else ever gave me sight.

Player: Were you afraid?

Man: Terrified.
Man: But also free.

-> END

# QUEST 4 – "The Story of the Good Samaritan"

## Story Setup
The player hears Jesus telling a story to a crowd.

## Objective
Understand what the parable means.

## Player Actions
- Listen carefully to the teaching
- Talk to listeners afterward
- Help an injured stranger in a side event
- Decide whether to sacrifice supplies

## Evidence Collected
- "Love Your Neighbour"

---

## Dummy Inky Conversation – Listener

=== start ===

Player: I don't understand the story.

Listener: That's the point of parables sometimes.

+ [Who was the hero?]
    Listener: The Samaritan.

+ [Why is that surprising?]
    Listener: Jews and Samaritans hated each other.

+ [So what was Jesus saying?]
    Listener: Your neighbour is anyone who needs mercy.

Player: Even enemies?

Listener: Especially enemies.

-> END

---

## Teen Explanation – The Good Samaritan

A man gets attacked and ignored by important religious people.

The only person who helps him is someone from a group people disliked.

Jesus teaches that:
- kindness matters more than status
- loving people includes outsiders
- real faith is shown through action

The story challenges prejudice.

---

## Dummy Inky Conversation – Disciple

=== start ===

Disciple: That perfume cost a fortune.

Player: Then why didn't Jesus stop her?

+ [Was it wasteful?]
    Disciple: Some of us thought so.

# QUEST 6 – "The Lost Son"

## Story Setup
A young listener asks the player what Jesus meant by a story about a rebellious son.

## Objective
Understand forgiveness.

## Player Actions
- Reconstruct the parable from listeners
- Help reunite two arguing brothers in a side quest
- Compare reactions from different NPCs

## Evidence Collected
- "God Forgives"

---

## Teen Explanation – The Lost Son (Prodigal Son)

A son demands his inheritance early, wastes it, and ruins his life.

When he returns home expecting punishment, his father runs to welcome him.

Jesus teaches:
- people can change
- forgiveness is powerful
- God wants people restored, not destroyed

The older brother in the story shows how jealousy and pride can stop people from showing mercy.

---

## Dummy Inky Conversation – Older Brother Listener

=== start ===

Player: Why was the older brother angry?

Listener: Because he thought the younger brother didn't deserve forgiveness.

+ [Wasn't he right?]
    Listener: Maybe.
    Listener: But Jesus wasn't talking about fairness.

+ [Then what was the point?]
    Listener: Mercy.

Player: That's hard.

Listener: Exactly.

-> END

=== start ===

Player: Is it true your brother died?

# QUEST 8 – "Render Unto Caesar"

## Story Setup
Religious leaders try to trap Jesus with political questions.

## Objective
Understand how Jesus answers impossible questions.

## Player Actions
- Observe debate in Temple courts
- Inspect Roman coin
- Speak to both Roman supporters and rebels
- Decide which group to trust

## Evidence Collected
- "Wisdom Under Pressure"

---

## Teen Explanation – Render Unto Caesar

People tried to trap Jesus.

If He rejected Roman taxes, Rome could arrest Him.
If He supported taxes, many Jews would hate Him.

Jesus answered:

"Give to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God."

He avoided the trap while teaching that:
- governments have authority
- but God should come first

## Objective

## Player Actions
- Observe wealthy donors

## Evidence Collected

# Conversation Design Rules

Conversations should:
- feel natural
- avoid overly religious language
- sound emotional and human
- let players ask questions teenagers would ask
- include uncertainty and disagreement

Not every witness should fully agree.

That makes the investigation more believable.

---

# Tone Guide for Teenagers

DO:
- use simple but meaningful language
- include emotional conflict
- allow humour sometimes
- make characters relatable
- focus on hope, fear, loyalty, doubt, justice

DON'T:
- preach at the player
- use overly complicated theology
- make every character sound perfect
- remove moral complexity

---

# Gameplay Systems Ideas

## Reputation System
Different groups react differently:
- disciples trust you more
- Temple leaders become suspicious
- Romans monitor you

## Curiosity Trait
The more deeply the player investigates:
- extra dialogue unlocks
- hidden witnesses appear
- deeper explanations become available

## Journal System
Player records:
- witness statements
- questions
- personal thoughts
- discovered meanings of parables

---

# Example Journal Entry

"Today I spoke to a man who claimed Jesus healed his blindness.

He seemed honest.
But some religious leaders called him a liar.

I still don't know what to believe.

But people don't usually risk everything for a fake story."

---

# End Goal of the Experience

The game should help teenagers:
- explore the historical setting
- understand Jesus's teachings
- think deeply about evidence and belief
- experience the emotions of the final week
- ask questions rather than feel preached at

The player's journey is not just about solving a mystery.

It's about discovering why Jesus changed so many lives.
