# Prophecies Fulfilled in Holy Week (Game Mapping)

This document provides a detailed breakdown of the prophecies fulfilled during Holy Week, as mapped to the specific acts and cases within the game. Each entry includes a summary of the prophecy, an explanation of its significance, and which in-game characters reference it.

---

## Act I: The Arrival

### **Case: `triumphal_entry` (The Missing Donkey)**

#### Zechariah 9:9
*   **Prophecy:** *"Behold, your king is coming to you; righteous and having salvation is he, humble and mounted on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey."*
*   **Explanation:** The donkey was a deliberate symbol of peace and humility, contrasting with the warhorses of conquering Roman rulers. Jesus's choice of mount was a public claim to be this prophesied humble king.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Peter** and **Tobias (the Owner)**, who both discuss the significance of the Zechariah scroll.

#### Psalm 118:25–26
*   **Prophecy:** *"Save us, we pray, O LORD! ... Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD!"*
*   **Explanation:** The crowd's cry of "Hosanna" is a direct quote from this messianic psalm. "Hosanna" is a plea for salvation ("Save us now!"), not just praise.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Jemimah (the Skeptic)** and **Tobias (the Owner)** both comment on the crowd's shouts.

#### Genesis 49:10–11
*   **Prophecy:** *"The scepter shall not depart from Judah... binding his foal to the vine and his donkey's colt to the choice vine."*
*   **Explanation:** Over 1,400 years prior, Jacob's blessing over the tribe of Judah described a royal figure associated with a donkey colt, establishing an ancient precedent for this messianic entry.
*   **Mentioned by:** This prophecy is part of the background lore discovered through the **"Two Disciples' Cloaks"** evidence.

#### Malachi 3:1
*   **Prophecy:** *"The Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple."*
*   **Explanation:** This prophecy connects the Triumphal Entry directly to the subsequent Temple visit and cleansing, framing them as a single, authoritative act of the Messiah arriving at His house.
*   **Mentioned by:** Referenced in the **"Pharisee's Written Complaint"** evidence, which shows the leadership understood the connection.

### **Case: `temple_cleansing` (The Overturned Tables)**

#### Malachi 3:1
*   **Prophecy:** *"The Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple... like a refiner's fire."*
*   **Explanation:** Malachi predicted the Messiah would come not to endorse the Temple system, but to purify it with judgment, like a fire refining metal.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Malachi (the Money Changer)** and **Marcus (the Guard)** both allude to the fiery intensity of Jesus's actions.

#### Isaiah 56:7
*   **Prophecy:** *"My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations."*
*   **Explanation:** By turning the Court of the Gentiles (the only area accessible to non-Jews) into a marketplace, the leaders had blocked the nations from worship. Jesus's action restored the court to its intended purpose.
*   **Mentioned by:** This is the core justification for the cleansing, discovered through the **"Shattered Dove Cages"** evidence.

#### Psalm 69:9
*   **Prophecy:** *"For zeal for your house has consumed me."*
*   **Explanation:** The disciples recognized that Jesus's action was not uncontrolled anger but a righteous, covenantal "zeal" for God's honor, just as David had.
*   **Mentioned by:** The disciples' understanding of this prophecy is a key part of the case's background lore.

### **Case: `fig_tree_incident` (The Barren Fig Tree)**

#### Micah 7:1
*   **Prophecy:** *"What misery is mine! I am like one who gathers summer fruit... there is no cluster of grapes to eat, no early fig I crave."*
*   **Explanation:** The prophet Micah used the image of a fruitless harvest to lament Israel's spiritual emptiness. Jesus's action with the fig tree was a living enactment of this prophetic disappointment.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Peter** and **John** both reflect on the tree's deceptive appearance versus its lack of fruit.

#### Jeremiah 8:13
*   **Prophecy:** *"There will be no grapes on the vine, no figs on the tree, and their leaves will wither."*
*   **Explanation:** Jeremiah prophesied that God would cause leaves to wither as a sign of judgment. The tree's immediate withering was a direct, visible fulfillment of this.
*   **Mentioned by:** The **"Withered Fig Tree (Tuesday)"** evidence directly links to this prophecy.

#### Psalm 33:8–9
*   **Prophecy:** *"For he spoke, and it came to be; he commanded, and it stood firm."*
*   **Explanation:** The event demonstrated Jesus's absolute authority over creation, where His spoken word has immediate physical effect, fulfilling the Psalmist's description of God's power.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Peter's** astonished reaction highlights his dawning recognition of this divine power.

#### Zechariah 4:6–7
*   **Prophecy:** *"'Not by might nor by power, but by my Spirit,' says the LORD Almighty. 'What are you, mighty mountain? ...you will become level ground.'"*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus used the "mountain-moving" metaphor, familiar from Zechariah, to teach that faith connects the believer to this same divine power that overcomes impossible obstacles.
*   **Mentioned by:** Jesus's teaching on faith, as recorded in the **"Jesus's Teaching on Faith"** evidence, is a direct reference to this concept.

## Act II: The Teacher

### **Case: `authority_challenged` (The Silenced Teacher)**

#### Psalm 118:22–23
*   **Prophecy:** *"The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus quoted this coronation psalm directly at the religious leaders ("the builders"), identifying Himself as the cornerstone of God's new work, which they were in the process of rejecting.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Caiaphas** and **Samuel (the Scribe)** both react to Jesus using this prophecy against them.

#### Isaiah 5:1–7
*   **Prophecy:** *"My beloved had a vineyard on a very fertile hill... but it yielded wild grapes."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus's Parable of the Wicked Tenants deliberately quoted Isaiah's "Song of the Vineyard." The religious leaders immediately recognized themselves as the unfaithful tenants.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Samuel (the Scribe)** notes the direct connection, and the **"Sketch of the Vineyard Parable"** evidence confirms it.

#### Daniel 7:13–14
*   **Prophecy:** *"There before me was one like a son of man, coming with the clouds of heaven... He was given authority, glory and sovereign power."*
*   **Explanation:** "Son of Man" was Jesus's preferred title for Himself. It was a direct claim to the divine authority described in Daniel's vision, a claim the High Priest understood as blasphemy.
*   **Mentioned by:** This is a core part of the theological background, understood by **Caiaphas** and the other leaders.

### **Case: `lazarus_plot` (The Price of Life)**

#### Isaiah 25:8
*   **Prophecy:** *"He will swallow up death forever; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces."*
*   **Explanation:** The resurrection of Lazarus served as a powerful, localized preview of the ultimate cosmic victory over death that this prophecy describes.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Martha of Bethany** and **Nicodemus** discuss the implications of this miracle, which directly fulfilled the spirit of Isaiah's promise.

### **Case: `olivet_discourse` (The End of the Age)**

#### Daniel 9:27
*   **Prophecy:** *"And at the temple he will set up an abomination that causes desolation."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus directly quoted Daniel's prophecy, applying it to the future destruction of the Temple in Jerusalem as a warning to His disciples.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Peter**, **John**, and **Thomas** all grapple with the meaning of this dire prediction.

#### Joel 2:30–31 & Isaiah 13:10
*   **Prophecy:** The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood; the stars of heaven will not show their light.
*   **Explanation:** Jesus incorporated this classic prophetic imagery of cosmic disturbances to describe the unmistakable signs that would herald His return.
*   **Mentioned by:** The disciples, particularly **John**, reflect on these ancient prophecies as they listen to Jesus.

## Act III: The Pressure Builds

### **Case: `last_supper` (The Broken Cup)**

#### Exodus 12:1–14
*   **Prophecy:** The Passover lamb must be an unblemished male, and its blood will be a sign for God to "pass over" the house.
*   **Explanation:** Jesus, the sinless Lamb of God, became the fulfillment of the 1,500-year-old Passover ritual on the very night He celebrated it.
*   **Mentioned by:** The entire case is built around this fulfillment, understood by **John Mark** as he records the events.

#### Jeremiah 31:31–34
*   **Prophecy:** *"I will make a new covenant... I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus inaugurated this New Covenant, foretold 600 years earlier, when He took the cup of wine and declared, "This is my blood of the new covenant."
*   **Mentioned by:** **John Mark** records these exact words in the **"Written Summary of Jesus's Words"** evidence.

#### Psalm 41:9
*   **Prophecy:** *"Even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted his heel against me."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus quoted this Psalm at the table just before giving the dipped bread to Judas, showing that even the betrayal was part of God's foreknown plan.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Judas** himself acknowledges this in his dialogue, recognizing that Jesus knew what was happening.

### **Case: `gethsemane_arrest` (The Severed Ear)**

#### Isaiah 53:7
*   **Prophecy:** *"He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; like a lamb that is led to the slaughter."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus fulfilled this by refusing to let His disciples fight, healing His enemy, and surrendering peacefully, even though He had the power to call legions of angels.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Simon Peter** and **Malchus** both express their astonishment that Jesus did not fight back.

#### Zechariah 13:7
*   **Prophecy:** *"Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus quoted this prophecy just before His arrest, predicting that His disciples would flee in fear, which they did.
*   **Mentioned by:** This is a key part of the background context for the disciples' fear and subsequent scattering.

### **Case: `sanhedrin_trial` (The Midnight Tribunal)**

#### Isaiah 50:6 & Micah 5:1
*   **Prophecy:** Offering His back to beatings, His face to mocking and spitting, and being struck on the cheek.
*   **Explanation:** The physical abuse Jesus endured at the hands of the Temple guards and council members was a direct fulfillment of these specific prophecies.
*   **Mentioned by:** The **"Priestly Robe Fragment"** is physical evidence of this abuse.

#### Psalm 27:12 & Psalm 35:11
*   **Prophecy:** False and malicious witnesses will rise up to testify against Him.
*   **Explanation:** The Sanhedrin's trial was a sham, relying on hired accusers whose testimonies contradicted each other, fulfilling the psalmist's prediction.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Ananias (the False Witness)** admits to being paid, and the **"Conflicting Depositions"** evidence proves the prophecy true.

### **Case: `barabbas_choice` (The People's Choice)**

#### Isaiah 53:3
*   **Prophecy:** *"He was despised and rejected by mankind, a man of suffering, and familiar with pain."*
*   **Explanation:** The prophecy was fulfilled in the most public way imaginable when the crowd literally voted to release a murderer and crucify the Messiah.
*   **Mentioned by:** The choice itself is the fulfillment, discussed by **Pontius Pilate** and **Barabbas**.

### **Case: `crucifixion_site` (The Final Sacrifice)**

#### Psalm 22:16–18
*   **Prophecy:** *"They have pierced my hands and my feet... they divide my garments among them and cast lots for my clothing."*
*   **Explanation:** Written 1,000 years before crucifixion was invented, this Psalm describes the event in precise detail, down to the soldiers gambling for His tunic.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Centurion Longinus** confirms his men were casting lots for the garments.

#### Amos 8:9
*   **Prophecy:** *"I will make the sun go down at noon and darken the earth in broad daylight."*
*   **Explanation:** A supernatural darkness covered the land for three hours, a cosmic sign that was astronomically impossible during a full-moon Passover.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Centurion Longinus** and **Pashhur (the Priest)** both testify to the unnatural darkness.

#### Isaiah 53:9
*   **Prophecy:** *"He was assigned a grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death."*
*   **Explanation:** Jesus died with criminals but was buried in the new, private tomb of a wealthy council member, Joseph of Arimathea, fulfilling the paradox.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Joseph of Arimathea** confirms he provided the tomb and purchased the fine linen.

#### Psalm 34:20
*   **Prophecy:** *"He protects all his bones; not one of them will be broken."*
*   **Explanation:** This prophecy, linked to the Passover lamb requirement (Exodus 12:46), was fulfilled when the soldiers pierced Jesus's side to confirm death instead of breaking His legs.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Centurion Longinus** explains he ordered the spear thrust, making the leg-breaking unnecessary.

## Act IV: The New Beginning

### **Case: `resurrection` (The Empty Tomb)**

#### Psalm 16:10
*   **Prophecy:** *"For you will not abandon my soul to Sheol, or let your holy one see corruption."*
*   **Explanation:** The resurrection on the third day, before the body could begin to decay, is the ultimate fulfillment of this promise. The empty tomb is its physical proof.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Joseph of Arimathea** reflects on this Psalm after seeing the empty tomb and the neatly folded grave clothes.

#### Hosea 6:2 & Jonah 1:17
*   **Prophecy:** Being revived on the third day; the "sign of Jonah" (three days in the earth).
*   **Explanation:** Jesus explicitly identified His resurrection with the "sign of Jonah." The timing—on the third day—was a consistent prophetic pattern.
*   **Mentioned by:** The **"Soldiers' Broken Report"** and the testimony of **Mary Magdalene** confirm the third-day event.

#### Isaiah 53:10–11
*   **Prophecy:** The Suffering Servant will be crushed, yet he shall "prolong his days" and "see light" after his anguish.
*   **Explanation:** This paradox within Isaiah's prophecy—a figure who is killed yet lives on—can only be resolved through resurrection.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Mary Magdalene's** testimony of seeing the risen Lord is the fulfillment of "seeing light" after suffering.

### **Case: `roman_inquiry` (The Guard's Report)**

#### Psalm 2:1–2
*   **Prophecy:** *"Why do the nations conspire and the peoples plot in vain? The kings of the earth rise up and the rulers band together against the LORD and against his anointed."*
*   **Explanation:** The Roman military and the Jewish Sanhedrin collaborating to create a cover-story for the empty tomb is a direct fulfillment of rulers conspiring to suppress God's truth.
*   **Mentioned by:** The **"High-Grade Sanctuary Coins"** (the bribe money) are the physical evidence of this conspiracy.

### **Case: `peter_restoration` (Peter's Restoration)**

#### Zechariah 13:7 & Ezekiel 34:11-16
*   **Prophecy:** The shepherd will be struck and the sheep scattered, but the Good Shepherd will seek, rescue, and feed His lost sheep.
*   **Explanation:** Peter's denial was a fulfillment of the "scattered sheep" prophecy. His restoration by the Sea of Galilee, where Jesus commissions him to "feed my sheep," is the fulfillment of the Good Shepherd gathering His flock.
*   **Mentioned by:** **Peter** himself recounts his threefold commission, which directly mirrors his threefold denial.
